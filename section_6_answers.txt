Section 6 - Advanced Questions Answers

Q1: Which emission sources have highest variability?
-----------------------------------------------------
Total_Energy_Consumption_kWh has the highest variability, followed by
NonRenewable_Energy_Consumption_kWh and Strategy_Implementation_Cost_USD.
This is shown by their high standard deviation values compared to other variables.


Q2: Are emissions normally distributed?
----------------------------------------
No, the emissions are NOT normally distributed. The normaltest returned a
p-value less than 0.05, which means we reject the null hypothesis of normality.
The distribution is slightly skewed.


Q3: Which variables are strongly correlated with emissions?
------------------------------------------------------------
Total_Energy_Consumption_kWh and NonRenewable_Energy_Consumption_kWh show
the strongest correlation with Carbon_Emission_tCO2e_TARGET.
Energy_Cost_USD and Raw_Material_Usage_kg also show moderate positive correlation.


Q4: Is there a trend in emissions?
------------------------------------
Yes, there is a slight increasing trend in carbon emissions over time.
The linear slope calculated from the monthly average emissions is positive,
indicating emissions are gradually rising across the observed period.


Q5: Do seasonal patterns exist?
---------------------------------
Yes, seasonal patterns exist. Some months show higher average emissions than others.
Mid-year months tend to have slightly higher emissions, which may be related to
increased industrial activity and energy consumption during those periods.
